Free and open source · Android 8.0+

Turns, calls and the time, on your Suzuki’s display.

Safar connects to the bike’s Bluetooth display and sends it turns from Google Maps, who is calling, WhatsApp calls and messages, the time and the phone’s battery. It also keeps a record of your riding: trips with their routes, fuel-ups and mileage, service reminders and where you parked.

The logbook

What Safar does

  1. Today

    The bike at a glance

    Connection status, the odometer, trip meters, fuel bars and engine temperature from the bike, and a garage of shortcuts.

    Safar reconnects on its own when you switch the ignition on.

  2. Ride mode

    Big enough to read on the move

    A large, glanceable screen that opens by itself when you start moving.

  3. Route

    Google Maps turns on the bike

    Start Google Maps navigation from your saved places. Each turn is shown on the bike’s display.

    Google Maps is the map. Safar reads its turn notification and passes it on.

  4. Alerts

    Who is calling, not what they wrote

    Phone calls, missed calls, WhatsApp calls and messages, each with its own switch. The bike only gets the caller’s name and a count, never the message text.

  5. Trips

    Every ride, written down

    Every ride with its day and start time, distance, speeds and a map of the route.

Also in the logbook

Fuel, service, papers

  • Fuel

    Log fill-ups, see your mileage and an estimated range, and get a low-fuel reminder.

  • Service

    Reminders for oil, chain, air filter, brakes and tyres, due by time, distance or both, whichever comes first. The first service can have its own odometer reading or date.

  • Parked

    The place your phone was when the bike switched off.

  • Papers

    Your RC, insurance, PUC, licence and service bills as PDFs or photos, with an optional expiry date. Safar reminds you 30 and 7 days before a paper runs out, and on the day.

  • Widget

    The connection, odometer, Trip A, range and the next turn on your home screen.

  • And in Settings

    • Export all your data to one .zip file, import it, or delete it.
    • Review every permission under Settings → All permissions.
    • Light and dark themes.

Bikes

Suzukis with a Bluetooth display

Safar recognises the display by its Bluetooth name. You don’t need a PIN to pair, and Safar reconnects on its own when you switch the ignition on.

Phone
Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer, with Bluetooth LE
Navigation
Google Maps
Permissions
Nearby devices, Notifications, Notification access, Location and Phone. Safar explains each one when you set it up.

Supported displays

  • V-Strom SXmotorcycle
  • Gixxer / Gixxer SFmotorcycle
  • Gixxer 250 / Gixxer SF 250motorcycle
  • Access 125scooter
  • Burgman Street / Street EXscooter
  • Avenisscooter

Privacy

Your data stays on your phone

0accounts

0servers

1way online: Google Maps, after you start a route

Everything is stored on the phone: a Room database for trips, fuel, service, places and papers, DataStore for settings, and the papers’ files in the app’s own storage.

The bike gets a caller’s name and a count. It never gets the text of a message.

Try it and build it

BikeSim

Try it without the bike

BikeSim advertises as a Suzuki display, accepts Safar’s frames and sends cluster readings every few seconds. Run it on a second phone, or run both apps on two emulators linked by virtual Bluetooth.

Build it yourself

You need JDK 25 for the Gradle daemon. The wrapper downloads Gradle 9.8.

$ ./gradlew :app:assembleDebug

To install a release build, sign it with your own keystore. The README explains how.
